## What I found

**Franchise bundles are the safer buy, by a modest margin.** Counting every film a studio would sell, the typical bundle earned 4.4 times its production cost against 3.8 for the typical standalone hit. From 2000 on it is 4.5 against 3.8. **The answer is the same in both windows** and every other way I measured it, and inflation cancels out because each film is compared with its own cost. The advantage is reliability: franchise films rarely do badly.

**Your list flatters franchises.** It holds mostly their famous films; counting the missing sequels cut the typical bundle's return by a fifth. Jaws looks like 67 times its cost; all four Jaws films earned 11 times.

## What I recommend

**Buy franchises selectively: complete bundles whose weakest film still performs.**

- **Franchises:** *The Lord of the Rings* (11 times cost; weakest film 9.4; all 3 films on your list). *The Avengers* (7.1; weakest 6.0; agree whether its 2 announced films are included). *Ice Age* (7.5 across all films; weakest 3.9).
- **Standalones:** *Spirited Away* (14.5, the best return since 2000), *Inception* (5.2) and *Interstellar* (4.5), the two largest audiences of any standalone, plus the older *Pulp Fiction* (26.7) and *Forrest Gump* (12.3), whose 31,000 viewer ratings each show interest today.
- **Talent:** Peter Jackson leads directors since 2000; Steven Spielberg and Robert Zemeckis lead overall, on older films. Tom Hanks is the steadiest actor; Christopher Nolan signals prestige, not box office. Most actors simply mirror their franchise.
- **Avoid as bundles:** Jaws, Hannibal Lecter, Ghostbusters and Jurassic Park are strong only on their originals.

**Deal note.** *The Lord of the Rings* is the only top bundle with every film on your list and no weak entry. It earned 11 times its cost against 4.4 for the typical bundle, so it is worth up to about 2.5 times the price of a typical franchise bundle. The number that drove my view is 9.4: its weakest film beat the typical film of every other franchise since 2000.
