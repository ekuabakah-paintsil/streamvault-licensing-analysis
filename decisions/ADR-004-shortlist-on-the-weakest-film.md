# ADR-004: Build the shortlist on each title's weakest film, not its best

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-19
- **Decision owner:** Ekua Micah Abakah-Paintsil

## Context

Kwame asked for three franchise targets, five standalones, a read on which directors and lead actors signal a strong title, and a deal note on the single strongest franchise. The brief does not say how to rank them. Kwame's team worries that StreamVault overpays for weak middle entries, and two years ago a trilogy bought on instinct underperformed.

- In the franchises with 3 or more films on the list, returns fall across the series: first films 7.76x, middle 5.41x, last 4.44x (all years).
- The bundle check showed that the films missing from the list are mostly later sequels, and several are weak: The Matrix's weakest film returned 0.82x, Mad Max's 1.04x, Indiana Jones's 1.30x.
- A bundle is only as safe as its weakest film, because it cannot be bought without it.

## Decision

Franchises are ranked on what the whole bundle returned and on its weakest film; standalones on a transparent score; talent on a minimum track record. Each pick carries the reason it made the cut.

1. **Franchise targets** must have 3 or more films on the list, all released from 2000 on, and are ranked on complete-bundle return and weakest film:
   - **The Lord of the Rings:** 10.96x, weakest film 9.37x, all 3 films on the list.
   - **Ice Age:** 7.50x complete (8.43x on the list's 3 films), weakest film 3.89x, 3 of 7 films on the list.
   - **The Avengers:** 7.13x, weakest film 5.98x, all 4 released films on the list; 2 more announced.
2. **Standalones**, a blend of three post-2000 films and two 1990s films with large current audiences: Spirited Away (14.47x), Inception (5.24x), Interstellar (4.52x), Pulp Fiction (26.74x, about 31,000 audience ratings) and Forrest Gump (12.32x, about 31,000 ratings).
3. **Standalone score:** 50% return, 25% audience rating (only for films with 50 or more ratings), 25% audience size, each as a percentile rank so no single blockbuster dominates.
4. **Talent:** directors, plus actors billed in the top 3, with at least 3 films on the list. Actor figures are read as franchise figures where the actor appears in one series only.
5. **Deal note:** priced relative to the typical complete bundle (4.36x), so The Lord of the Rings is worth about 2.5 times the typical bundle. The one number is 9.37x, its weakest film.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: Rank franchises on their best film or their average

- **How it would work:** Shortlist the franchises with the highest single return or the highest average.
- **Why it is attractive:** It surfaces the most famous, highest-earning titles.
- **Why rejected:** It would put Jaws first at 67x. The whole Jaws bundle returned 11.3x and its weakest film 2.3x. Ranking on the best film repeats exactly the instinct buy that hurt Kwame two years ago.

### Alternative B: Include pre-2000 franchises

- **How it would work:** Allow franchises released before 2000, such as Back to the Future (9.79x, all 3 films on the list, weakest film 6.13x).
- **Why it is attractive:** Back to the Future is complete and strong, and would rank second.
- **Why rejected:** All three shortlist picks must survive Yaw in the room. Back to the Future is named in the memo as the reserve if pre-2000 titles are acceptable.

### Alternative C: Harry Potter in place of Ice Age

- **How it would work:** Pick the larger, better-rated franchise (8 films, all on the list, rated 7.77 against Ice Age's 6.78).
- **Why it is attractive:** Brand strength and a complete bundle.
- **Why rejected:** Lower return (6.06x) and a weak film at 3.74x across 8 entries: more opportunities for the "weak middle entry" problem the team is worried about.

### Alternative D: The five best standalones by score alone

- **How it would work:** Pulp Fiction, Forrest Gump, Spirited Away, Schindler's List, Inception.
- **Why it is attractive:** The strongest numbers.
- **Why rejected:** Three of five would rest on 1990s box office. The blend keeps two 1990s films only where about 31,000 current audience ratings each (as many as Inception) show present-day interest, which Yaw's concern about old box office does not touch. The Good, the Bad and the Ugly has the highest return (32x) but is excluded: a 1966 figure on a $1.2M budget is the most attackable number on the list, and its audience is the smallest of the contenders.

## Consequences

- **What this buys:** A shortlist built on the downside, which is the risk Kwame was burned by. The Lord of the Rings carries no hidden films and no weak entry. Negotiators get named missing sequels, weak entries and announced films to use at the table. The talent read is honest about which actors are really franchise proxies: Mark Hamill and Carrie Fisher are Star Wars, and Daniel Radcliffe, Rupert Grint and Emma Watson are Harry Potter.
- **What this costs:** The score weights are my judgement, not a fact. A minimum of 3 films is a thin basis for talent claims, and the strongest directors (Steven Spielberg, 13.25x over 8 films; Robert Zemeckis, 10.32x over 4) worked mostly before 2000. Peter Jackson (6.73x over 6 films) is the strongest signal from 2000 on; Christopher Nolan (7 films) is rated highest (8.21) but returns only 4.45x, so he signals prestige rather than box office.
- **What it costs the client if I am wrong:** Every return is box office against production budget, not against what a studio will charge StreamVault, and box office is a proxy for audience pull, not streaming value. If licence prices do not track these returns, the premium in the deal note is the wrong size, though the order of the shortlist would likely hold. Ice Age's weakest film is 3.89x, so its floor is the lowest of the three. The Avengers has two announced films; if the bundle terms include them at today's price, their value is unknown.
- **What would make me revisit this:** Licence prices becoming available, the Avengers terms including unreleased films, or a rerun moving a shortlisted franchise below the typical complete bundle.

## What this survives

Adjoa rerunning this next year. The ranking rules are code: the same rules applied to 500 titles produce a new shortlist without a rewrite, and the complete-bundle check updates automatically. What does not survive automatically is judgement. The blend of standalones and the reading of talent figures will need a person to look at next year's list, and these rules are the starting point for that, not a replacement for it.
